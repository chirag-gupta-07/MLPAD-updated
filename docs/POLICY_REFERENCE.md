# MPLADS Policy Reference Document

This document summarizes key provisions of the MPLADS Guidelines 2016 (as amended)
and the General Financial Rules (GFR) 2017 relevant to the AI Risk Intelligence Platform.

## MPLADS Guidelines 2016 — Fund Release and Utilization

### Para 3.1 — Annual Entitlement
Each Member of Parliament is entitled to recommend works to the tune of Rs. 5 crore per annum under the MPLADS scheme. The funds are released by the Government of India directly to the District Authority in two instalments of Rs. 2.5 crore each.

### Para 3.2 — Release Conditions
The second instalment shall not be released unless the District Authority furnishes the Utilisation Certificate (UC) for the first instalment and at least 60% of the cumulative funds released (including the current year's first instalment) have been utilised.

### Para 3.5 — Lapsing of Funds
Funds under MPLADS are non-lapsable. The unutilised amount in a particular year is carried forward to the next year. However, any amount remaining unutilised for more than 3 consecutive years shall be reviewed by the Ministry.

## MPLADS Guidelines 2016 — Work Recommendation and Sanction

### Para 4.1 — Recommendation by MP
The MP shall recommend works to the District Authority in writing. The recommendation should specify the nature of work, location, estimated cost, and the implementing agency.

### Para 4.2 — Sanction by District Authority
The District Authority (District Collector/Deputy Commissioner/District Magistrate) shall sanction the recommended works after verifying their eligibility, technical feasibility, and cost reasonableness within 75 days of receipt of the recommendation.

### Para 4.3 — Cost Ceiling per Work
The maximum permissible cost of a single work under MPLADS is Rs. 75 lakh. Works costing more than Rs. 75 lakh can be sanctioned only with the prior approval of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).

### Para 4.5 — Prohibited Works
The following categories of works shall NOT be sanctioned under MPLADS: (a) works on private land or property, (b) construction of religious structures, (c) works benefiting individual private persons, (d) purchase of inventory items without a permanent community asset, (e) recurring maintenance expenditure, (f) works within cantonment areas without prior defence clearance.

## MPLADS Guidelines 2016 — Implementation and Monitoring

### Para 5.1 — Implementing Agency Selection
The District Authority shall select the implementing agency from among government departments, local bodies, or reputed NGOs with adequate technical capacity. The agency should have a proven track record of completing similar works.

### Para 5.2 — Tender and Contract Process
For works costing more than Rs. 5 lakh, competitive bidding through e-procurement is mandatory. For works costing Rs. 5 lakh or less, limited tender or quotation may be adopted. All procurement must follow GFR 2017 rules.

### Para 5.3 — Completion Timeline
All works sanctioned under MPLADS should be completed within 12 months from the date of sanction. Extension beyond 12 months requires justification and approval from the District Authority with reasons recorded in writing.

### Para 5.4 — Quality Assurance and Inspection
The District Authority shall ensure quality inspection at key milestones: (a) foundation stage, (b) at 50% completion, and (c) final completion. Third-party quality audit is recommended for works exceeding Rs. 25 lakh.

## MPLADS Guidelines 2016 — Financial Management

### Para 6.1 — Single Nodal Account
All MPLADS funds shall be maintained in a single designated bank account at the district level. The District Authority is the custodian of these funds.

### Para 6.2 — Payment Through PFMS
All payments to implementing agencies and contractors under MPLADS shall be made through the Public Financial Management System (PFMS) for end-to-end tracking and transparency.

### Para 6.3 — Advance Payment Limits
Advance payment (mobilization advance) to a contractor shall not exceed 10% of the sanctioned amount. Subsequent payments shall be released milestone-wise against measurable physical progress certified by the implementing agency.

### Para 6.4 — Utilisation Certificate
The District Authority shall furnish annual Utilisation Certificates (UCs) to MoSPI by the 30th of June following the financial year, certifying the expenditure incurred and physical progress achieved for all works.

## GFR 2017 — Procurement Rules

### Rule 144 — Open Tender Enquiry
Procurement of goods and services valued above Rs. 25 lakh shall be through advertised tender enquiry (open competitive bidding) published on the Central Public Procurement Portal (CPPP) and GeM.

### Rule 149 — Evaluation of Tenders
Tenders shall be evaluated on the basis of eligibility, technical qualification, and financial bid. The contract shall be awarded to the lowest evaluated responsive bidder (L1). Deviations from L1 must be justified and recorded.

### Rule 170 — e-Procurement
All ministries and departments shall use e-procurement for goods and services valuing Rs. 10 lakh and above. This mandate applies to MPLADS works through the respective state e-procurement portals.

### Rule 173(xxiv) — Integrity Pact
For procurement above Rs. 20 crore, an Integrity Pact shall be executed between the buyer and the bidders committing to transparency and non-bribery. This clause also applies to high-value MPLADS works requiring MoSPI approval.

## GFR 2017 — Financial Control

### Rule 130 — Assessment of Financial Propriety
Every officer incurring or authorising expenditure from public funds shall ensure that: (a) the expenditure is sanctioned by the competent authority, (b) sufficient budget provision is available, (c) the expenditure is not prima facie more than the occasion demands.

### Rule 133 — Internal Financial Controls
Each ministry shall maintain adequate internal financial controls to ensure: (a) orderly and efficient conduct of business, (b) safeguarding of assets, (c) prevention and detection of errors and fraud, (d) accuracy and completeness of accounting records.

### Rule 23 — Delegation of Financial Powers
Financial powers are delegated to various levels of government functionaries. At the district level, the District Collector may sanction works up to the limits prescribed by the state government and MPLADS Guidelines.
